Britain's Youth Jobs Plan Arrives With a Bill Attached

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

Milburn's verdict lands tomorrow, the NHS revolts over zero-hours, and an AI can now pay for things.

UK weather

Derbyshire: Today sunny (2–7°C); Tuesday heavy rain (6–9°C); Wednesday light rain (6–9°C). BBC Weather, Derby

UK news

Burnham promises a “root-and-branch rethink of childhood” ahead of Milburn’s report — Alan Milburn publishes his review into young people and work tomorrow, and the prime minister has spent the weekend setting expectations for it, pledging early intervention years before anyone signs on and a “benefits backstop” alongside nearly half a million job and education opportunities over a decade. Almost a million young people are not in education, employment or training, so the political question is no longer whether the benefits bill gets reshaped but who absorbs the cost of it. Guardian

NHS and fire chiefs tell ministers the zero-hours ban puts the public at risk — Health and fire service leaders have joined forces to warn that outlawing zero-hours contracts would leave them unable to hire emergency cover without guaranteeing hours or paying for late shift changes, with NHS Employers putting the annual bill for trusts at as much as £900m. They want a rethink before the rules come in next year; the government says it is considering consultation responses. A manifesto promise meeting a rota, which never ends well for the rota. LBC

UK television

I’m A Celebrity returns tonight — Ant and Dec are back in Australia for the launch at 9pm on ITV1 and ITVX, with last year’s run having averaged 8.7m viewers and reached 23.4m people across platforms. A show now in its third decade as the closest thing British telly has to a fixed point in the calendar. Vibe 107.6

Channel 4 gives Nicola Coughlan two nights to herself — “I Am Helen”, a two-part drama, starts at 9pm tonight and concludes tomorrow, part of a week where the schedules quietly fill up with prestige drama ahead of December. Geektown

Pokémon

The Pokémon Company is counting down to something, and the clock expires on 30 November — Logos numbered 1,025, 843 and then 676 have appeared on its Japanese account, each posted precisely that many hours before 14:00 UTC on the 30th, which is a strange and slightly wonderful way to tease an announcement. A “Winds and Waves” date is the obvious guess; the text underneath reads, roughly, “Continue with Pokémon.” Polygon

Delta Reign leads a crowded November for the card game — Mega Evolution’s Delta Reign expansion landed at the start of the month, and the rest of November is a run of 30th Celebration collector sets and the Mega Greninja ex deck, timed for the anniversary and the Christmas gift market in roughly equal measure. Pokémon

Tabletop games

The 11th Edition Ork codex has leaked, and the new Battlewagon rumour finally has legs — Ork players are looking at leaked codex pages plus fresh Nobz and a Battlewagon redo, arriving in a release window that is now essentially here. Treat leaked rules as gossip until Games Workshop says otherwise, but the shape of an Ork refresh is getting harder to mistake for wishful thinking. Spikey Bits

Fiends & Foes lands 24 November — Erin M. Evans writes a bestiary of beasts, villains and monstrosities from across the planes, published through Ten Speed Press. D&D’s year of thin rulebook releases has left a gap for exactly this kind of lavishly illustrated monster book. Screen Rant

Sci-fi and fantasy

“We Are The Borg” is a Commander deck now, which is either perfect or terrifying — The Star Trek Universes Beyond set for Magic: The Gathering is out, and one of its four Commander decks is led by the Borg Queen and Locutus of Borg, a White-Blue-Black Esper build whose whole plan is to take your opponents’ creatures and assimilate them. Resistance remains, as ever, futile; the mana base is the interesting part. Screen Rant

Tech

AI — OpenAI wants your agent to have a wallet, with your thumb on the trigger — ChatGPT Wallet lets an agent complete a purchase after you approve the items, total and card via Face ID or fingerprint, with the payment network substituting a temporary card number so neither the agent nor the merchant ever sees the real one. Built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, it starts with US Pro subscribers, and the planned next step is pre-approved spending limits. Every safeguard here is a good idea and every one will eventually be described as friction. OpenAI

AI — the Fed now counts AI as its biggest financial stability worry — 87% of contacts in the Federal Reserve’s latest survey named AI-related risk, up from 50% in May, citing complex financing arrangements, cyber threats and the possibility of a correction if AI earnings disappoint. A central bank describing your industry as its top systemic concern is the sort of citation nobody puts on the website. Reuters

Linux — a busy week ends with Firefox 160, Steam’s 64-bit move and RHEL 10.3 — Firefox 160 ships Mini Window, MathML in PDFs and playback-speed settings for picture-in-picture; Steam’s client moved to 64-bit support; and Red Hat released RHEL 10.3 on the 6.12 kernel with GCC 16 and sealed images. Quietly, most of the desktop stack changed underneath everyone in seven days. 9to5Linux

Android — a week after the Pixel Drop, plenty of Pixels still haven’t got it — Google announced the November update on the 10th, and owners are still reporting no OTA, with the rollout apparently staged over several weeks. Those who do have it are on Android 17 QPR2 with roughly 33 functional fixes and a batch of new features. A staged rollout is defensible until it’s your phone waiting. Android Authority

macOS — the 27.3 beta cycle rolls on — Apple has seeded 27.3 betas across iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, visionOS and watchOS for developers, with the public release of 27.3 not expected until December. Meanwhile 27.2.1, out on the 9th, remains the version to actually run. Apple Developer

Hardware — the smallest Meshtastic node gets a printable case — Seeed Studio’s XIAO ESP32S3 paired with a Wio-SX1262 is about as small as a usable LoRa node gets, and there’s now a 3D-printable enclosure for it that doubles as a sensor-node housing or a single-channel gateway. The sort of project that costs an evening and then quietly lives in a coat pocket for years. Adafruit

The week’s unifying theme is approval: the agent asks before spending, the PM asks before reshaping benefits, the NHS asks before the rota changes, and the Fed asks whether anyone has thought this through. The Borg deck does not ask, which is why it’s the healthiest of the four.
