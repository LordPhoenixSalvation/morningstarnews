Today’s Edition

Ground Rent, Meet the Peppercorn

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

Leasehold is to be abolished in all but name, the Bayesian inquiry finds a risk nobody had assessed, and Anthropic goes public at a number that stops conversation.

UK NEWS

‘Feudal’ leasehold system set to be scrapped in England and Wales — The Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill is published today and introduced to Parliament, banning most new leasehold flats in favour of commonhold, where residents collectively own the building. Ground rent for existing leaseholders is capped at £250 from 2028 and falls to a peppercorn after 40 years. Housing minister Matthew Pennycook called the current system “anachronism in the 21st century”; freeholders, including housing associations and the pension funds holding the ground rents, call it an “unprecedented abuse” of their rights that will wipe billions off portfolios. It will not be law before next year at the earliest, and both Houses get a say first. BBC News

Crew unaware of risks before Mike Lynch’s yacht Bayesian sank, say investigators — The Marine Accident Investigation Branch’s final report finds the superyacht had a vulnerability to knockdown that had never been assessed with its sails down at anchor, so nobody aboard knew how easily she could go over in the squall that killed seven people off Sicily in August 2024, including Mike Lynch and his daughter Hannah. The centreboard is at the centre of it. The findings sit awkwardly beside the Italian criminal inquiry, which has taken a different view of who was responsible. BBC News

Anglo-Saxon ‘sand body’ among new Sutton Hoo finds — Time Team’s dig in the Garden Field cemetery has uncovered a grave where the body survives not as bone but as an impression pressed into the sand, along with beads, tweezers, a knife and a brooch, and evidence of a second burial beneath it. The excavation is the first serious work beyond the royal mounds in decades, and it suggests the cemetery was far busier than the famous ship burial implied. BBC News

Derbyshire

Work begins on Swadlincote’s new leisure centre and council HQ — Construction has started at Cadley Park, on the former Cadley Colliery site off William Nadin Way, for South Derbyshire District Council’s new leisure centre and headquarters, with completion pencilled in for the end of 2028. A derelict pit yard becoming the council’s own front door is the sort of quiet regeneration that either works or becomes a decade-long story; the district is betting on the first. BBC News

UK TELEVISION

Bob Mortimer’s The Satsuma Complex is becoming a Disney+ drama — Five parts, from Quay Street Productions, with Mortimer executive producing alongside Nicola Shindler. Gary Thorn, a lonely legal assistant in Peckham, meets a woman in a pub, she vanishes leaving only his book behind, and by morning his colleague Brendan is dead. Mortimer’s own statement: “I applied for a job but was told my face had become ‘slightly stale’.” No transmission date yet. TV Zone UK

Saturday Night Live UK returns on 16 January — 10pm on Sky One, with the returning cast joined by Freddie Meredith. Live, weekly, written in the week it airs, which is the entire point and also the entire risk. TV Zone UK

POKEMON

Pokémon and Duolingo have done a deal, and it runs to 21 December — A seven-day Duolingo streak unlocks Timed Research in Pokémon GO that lets you pick a language and receive Pokémon carrying that language tag with special backgrounds; the Duolingo app gets Pokémon costumes for your profile in return. It is the strangest crossover of the anniversary year, and the only one that might accidentally teach somebody a verb. Serebii

TABLETOP GAMES

Space Marines have lost their last aircraft, and every other flyer is now on notice — The new codex sends the Stormhawk Interceptor, Stormtalon Gunship and Stormraven Gunship to Legends in one stroke, and Games Workshop has said this is how removals will work from now on. Every codex on the 11th edition schedule is therefore another chance for somebody’s plane to go the same way, and Ork players should read the Heldrake precedent closely: the dataslate stripped its Aircraft keyword, set its OC to zero and gave it a normal 12” move rather than retiring the model. If a Dakkajet can be quietly turned into a very fast, very ugly ground vehicle, it will be. Spikey Bits

GW has capped allocations on the Christmas Battleforces everyone actually wants — Retailers say the Chaos Knights Helhunt Lance, Chaos Space Marines Cult of Destruction and the two Horus Heresy battle groups are being limited per store, while sales reps ring round offering extra copies of the rest after the 4 November order deadline. The Orks Armoured Blitzmob — Mega Armoured Warboss, Battlewagon, Deff Dread, Meganobz and Boyz — is not on the short list, so it should be findable. The army that just anchored a new edition being the easy one to buy is its own small indignity. Spikey Bits

SCI-FI AND FANTASY

Captain Seven’s flagship series reaches issue two — IDW’s ongoing Star Trek, written by Christopher Cantwell with Dennis Menheere on art, picks up the Enterprise-G on a desperate run beyond the known galaxy following the events of Picard. It is out this week, alongside the penultimate issue of the weekly 60th-anniversary miniseries. For a franchise between television seasons, the comics are currently doing the actual storytelling. TrekMovie

First look at Sutherland, the BBC’s far-north space thriller — Gugu Mbatha-Raw plays flight director Mirren Wood, running the UK’s first crewed mission from a privately funded spaceport in the near future, while Stuart Martin’s DS Logan Muir investigates a missing biologist who came back and tried to break into the site. Filming has wrapped in Scotland; Michael McElhatton joins as the head of the Paravel Space Hub. No date yet. TV Zone UK

TECH

AI — Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share and raised $81.3bn, the largest stock market debut on record, valuing the five-year-old company at $1.5tn ahead of trading on the Nasdaq today. The offering was upsized, which is the detail that matters: demand was not the problem. BBC News

Linux — 7.4-rc3 is out, a quiet round dominated by networking and GPU driver fixes plus a large iomap calling-convention change, with the final release still pointing at late December. The merge window behind it was the largest on record at 19,671 commits, so a calm rc3 is better news than it sounds. Phoronix

Android — The November Google System Updates are landing, and the interesting one is the Pixel virtual gamepad, which finally gives foldables a sensible way to play. Play Store, Play services and Wallet changes ride alongside, plus a new family space in Settings. 9to5Google

Hardware — ESP-IDF v6.1.1 shipped on Monday, a maintenance release on the 6.1 line for anyone building against Espressif’s toolchain. Nothing dramatic; update if you are on 6.1, and note the Meshtastic firmware beta has not moved since June. ESP-IDF releases

So: the law arrives to end a system it will take forty years to unwind, the report arrives to explain a sinking nobody could have avoided because nobody had been told, and a five-year-old company is now worth more than the entire stock market of most countries. Everything today is paperwork catching up with something that already happened.
