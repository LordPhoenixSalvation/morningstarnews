Complete Reset, With a Bill Attached

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

Milburn's review lands in full, snow on the Peak District, and Google breaks its own cameras.

UK weather

Derbyshire: Today heavy rain (5–9°C); Wednesday light rain (5–8°C); Thursday thick cloud (1–8°C). BBC Weather, Derby

UK NEWS

Milburn’s review is published, and it says 440,000 more young people need work — The report trailed all week arrived this morning, and it is blunter than the pre-briefings suggested: a “complete system reset”, a legal entitlement to support through a new Youth Opportunity Service in England, a named adviser within four months, and a target of getting the Neet rate from 13% down to around 5% in a decade. The money comes from young adults losing the Universal Credit health top-up and Pip, replaced by a Youth Allowance and an Additional Cost Allowance. Milburn’s own arithmetic is the striking bit: for every £1 spent helping young people into work last year, the state spent £25 on youth benefits. He wants that at £2 to £1 by 2036. BBC News

The Met’s rape unit kept a predator on the payroll — A detective who abused women was allowed to keep working in a specialist rape unit while complaints stacked up around him, with the force’s own systems recording the behaviour becoming more extreme rather than triggering an intervention. It is the most-read story on the BBC this morning, which tells you something about how the public reads these cases now — not as individual failures but as a pattern with a management structure attached. BBC News

Derbyshire

A snow warning that starts at Buxton — The Met Office’s yellow warning for snow runs until 11am and its western edge is Buxton, which means the Peak District is inside it while Derby gets heavy rain at 5–9°C. Five to fifteen centimetres above 300–400 metres, snow down to about 100 metres in places, and gusts of 40–50mph over high ground that could bring branches and power lines down. The UK Health Security Agency has cold health alerts running for northern England until 09:00 Thursday. If you’re driving over the tops this morning, this is the one to read before you set off. BBC Weather

UK TELEVISION

The Witch Farm gets Sunday night on BBC One — A new drama taking the 9pm slot on 22 November, which is the sort of placement the BBC only gives something it expects to be talked about on Monday. Same week: Lucy Worsley’s Keys To The Castle starts on BBC Two on the Monday, and Channel 4 has The Plot To Kidnap Holly Willoughby in the same 9pm slot — a factual clash worth noting, since both are chasing the same audience with very different registers. TV Zone

Sweetpea is back, and Sky is putting it on Atlantic — The second series lands Thursday 26 November at 9pm. It is the week’s biggest returning title and it is buried on a subscription channel, which is either confidence or the opposite. TV Zone

POKEMON

Duolingo and Pokémon GO have actually shipped the thing — Duo’s Pokémon Party went live this morning at 10:00 JST: do a lesson a day for seven days, get an offer code for Timed Research in GO, with Pikachu, Meowth, Chatot and Lucario carrying special backgrounds. The genuinely new mechanic is the language tag — event Pokémon get a summary-page toggle that shows their name in other languages, which is the most Pokémon thing imaginable and also quietly useful for a game with players in fourteen languages. Pokémon GO

TABLETOP GAMES

Khemri marches again, and the Night Goblins take the Blood Bowl pitch — Saturday’s pre-orders are a two-game week: Tomb Kings of Khemri reinforcements for The Old World, and a Night Goblin Blood Bowl team which, going by the previews, comes with the squigs and the tricks you would expect and rather more of both than is sensible. Black Library has a substantial slate alongside it. For Ork players it is a quiet week — the warband stays in the box until the 11th Edition codex cycle moves again. Warhammer Community

SCI-FI AND FANTASY

Two Star Trek books land today, and they are for the people who were here first — Una McCormack’s The Peacemakers is a Deep Space Nine novel about Jake Sisko, five years after the Dominion War, writing a book about his own experiences and finding that both Starfleet and Bajor want a piece of him — and that rumours about his father’s past could undo him. Alongside it, the Kelvin Era IDW collection. With no Trek series in production for the first time in a decade, the books are where the continuity is still being written. Memory Alpha

TECH

AI — GitHub has deprecated several Copilot models as of yesterday: Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Terra are gone, with Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol/Luna pointed at instead. If you have CI or agent workflows pinning a specific model, they may be silently falling back rather than failing, which is the worse of the two outcomes. AI Agent Store

Android — Google’s November update, which reached Pixels early and is still rolling out, is leaving cameras unusable on some devices running Android 17, alongside reports of broken always-on display, lock screens and notifications. The security patch is fine; the functional update is the problem. Android Police

Hardware — ESP-IDF v6.1.1 shipped yesterday, and it is a bugfix release for 6.1 rather than a feature drop, but two of the fixes matter if you build anything: esp_https_ota now refuses plain HTTP unless you explicitly opt back in, and the ESP HTTP server had a Content-Length truncation fixed to prevent request smuggling — plus a new HTTPD_MAX_REQ_BODY_LEN_KB cap defaulting to 16MB, which will break anything posting larger bodies without a config change. There is also a PSRAM XIP boot failure fixed for ESP32-P4 applications built with 5.5.2–5.5.4. If you are on 5.5.x, this is the release that stops the CHIP_LP_WDT_RESET loop. ESP-IDF releases

The country has spent a week being told a report was coming, and this morning it arrived with a bill attached — £25 on benefits for every £1 on work, currently, and a decade to turn that round. Meanwhile the weather did the thing British weather does, which is to be snow on the hills and rain in the valley on the same morning, and Google shipped an update that broke the camera. Everything in public life is a ratio somebody is trying to move.
