Petrol Pushes Inflation Back Up, and Nobody Wants to Say the Word

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

A 12-month high in the cost of living, a river you cannot swim in, and a great many websites quietly serving malware through a tool they all trusted.

UK weather

Derbyshire: Today light rain (5–9°C); Thursday light rain (2–8°C); Friday sunny intervals (-3–8°C). BBC Weather, Derby

UK NEWS

Inflation climbs to 3.6%, a 12-month high, and the pumps are to blame — The Consumer Prices Index rose 3.6% in the year to October, up from 3.5% in September, the ONS said this morning. Petrol went up 5.2p a litre between September and October to 174.7p, the highest since August 2022. Food and services were the drag the other way; fuel did the damage. The Bank of England meets next month with the rate still above target and the direction of travel now sideways rather than down. BBC News

Britain’s worst bathing site, and the sewage works next door — The BBC sampled the water upstream of French Weir Park in Taunton, named the worst in the country with a 68% failure rate for faecal bacteria, and found the treated liquid coming out of the nearest works at Bradford-on-Tone carrying around 240,000 colony forming units of E. coli per 100ml. Treated, in this context, is doing a great deal of work. Wessex Water says it may be agricultural. Both things are probably true. BBC News

Derbyshire

Derbyshire Police admit officers unlawfully entered a couple’s home and assaulted them — A couple who called police about a guest with a knife at their own house party ended up arrested themselves when officers forced entry. The charges were thrown out mid-trial when the force’s own evidence established the entry was unlawful; the couple then brought a civil claim and have been awarded £9,000 in damages. The force has apologised. Nearly four years on. BBC News

UK TELEVISION

Strike returns for the sixth time, and this one is The Running Grave — BBC One has confirmed the new series for Sunday 6 December at 9pm, in the schedules for 5–11 December. The Suffolk-set adaptation lands in the pre-Christmas fortnight, which is either a vote of confidence or a way of burying it under Strictly. TV Zone UK

ITV has published its Christmas highlights — Across ITV1 and ITVX through December. No dates worth circling yet, but the brochure is out, which means the full schedules with times follow in early December and the argument about what’s on at 3pm can begin properly. Where to Watch

POKEMON

Pokémon Champions gets its first balance update on 2 December — Announced today, arriving after scheduled server maintenance, with the patch notes already partly out. First real pass at the competitive roster since launch. My Nintendo News

TABLETOP GAMES

Dungeons & Dragons: World of Warcraft is out, and it is the first Universes Beyond book — A 256-page gameplay expansion for the 2024 rules, with the Icecrown Citadel map pack, DM screen and miniatures shipping alongside it. Azeroth translated into fifth edition, which is either a natural fit or the beginning of the end, depending on which forum you read. Vice

Nearly 200 sites, D&D Beyond among them, served card-skimming malware through Optimizely — An attacker injected code into the “Project JavaScript” panel of customer projects on the website-testing platform, and because it came from Optimizely’s own trusted servers every affected site loaded it. Report URI counted 178 organisations: Zoom, the BBC, CNN, Salesforce, IKEA, HSBC, and Wizards of the Coast’s Magic storefront Secret Lair. The script was updated 26 times across the day, ending up swapping crypto wallet addresses, skimming card fields and pushing exploits at older iPhones. Optimizely patched it and the clean-up finished by the evening of the 16th. Wizards has said nothing publicly. If you bought anything on D&D Beyond on Monday, check your statement. D&D Fanatics

SCI-FI AND FANTASY

Star Trek Online brings back Return to Duty for console players — The Starter Pack is on Xbox and PlayStation from 14 to 24 November at 1,500 Zen, with three other bundles alongside it: Undiscovered, Herald and Frontier Day. The Kobayashi Maru Supply Device is the one people actually want. A 60th-anniversary year that has been, to put it gently, uneven, ending the way it began — with Cryptic selling you the same ship again. Screen Rant

TECH

AI — OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, and its own system card is the story: both sit at the Critical cybersecurity threshold, both at High for biological and chemical risk, and Astra also reaches High for AI self-improvement. Default restrictions on higher-risk cyber assistance are tighter, and more permissive access now needs separate vetting. Microsoft came out the same day with MAI Code 2 and licensed model weights for Azure customers. AI Impact Hub

Linux — Greg Kroah-Hartman pushed three stable kernels today: 7.3.5, 6.18.64 and 6.12.118. If you run 7.2, note it went end-of-life on 1 November, so the upgrade path is 7.3. Tux Machines

Android — A week after the November Pixel Drop was announced, plenty of owners still do not have it, and the features that define it were the missing part rather than the security patch. Gemini Call Screen, HiLight and the rest are still rolling. Android Authority

So: the fuel that heats the country and moves the goods costs more than it did, the water it flows into fails a basic hygiene test, and the platforms we trust to sell us dice and rules quietly loaded somebody else’s script into 200 browsers at once. Everything this morning is a bill arriving from a system someone else was supposed to be maintaining.
