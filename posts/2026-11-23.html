Today’s Edition

Tear gas, or something like it, empties a Manchester arena

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

A major incident at Co-op Live, the NHS hands 700,000 mammograms to a machine, and Star Trek's best film turns thirty

UK weather

Derbyshire: Today sunny (1–7°C); Tuesday thick cloud (2–7°C); Wednesday light cloud (1–6°C). BBC Weather, Derby

UK NEWS

Co-op Live emptied by an unknown substance - Greater Manchester Police declared a major incident shortly after 10pm on Sunday when something was released into the crowd near the end of Don Toliver’s set at the 12,000-capacity arena. One person was taken to hospital and around fifty were assessed by paramedics; police have not yet said what the substance was, though tear gas was the first assumption. source

The NHS hands the world’s biggest AI screening trial its first mammograms - Women at thirty sites, 700,000 of them over the next few years, will have one of the two radiologists who normally read each scan replaced by software. It is a £10m trial run by Warwick and Cambridge rather than a rollout, and the point is the shortage of readers as much as the promise of the model. source

Poundland is being prepared for mass closures - With rescue talks fading, plans are reportedly being drawn up to shut a large slice of the 600-store estate, which employs about 12,000 people. The chain has spent a fortnight living on court extensions and its owner’s released pocket money. source

Derbyshire

Police warn after a stone goes through a patrol car’s windscreen on the M1 - Derbyshire Police say the vehicle was hit near the county’s stretch of the motorway and have urged drivers to report anyone seen throwing objects from bridges or verges. It follows a spate of brick and stone attacks on vehicles in the area that has already produced arrests. source

UK TELEVISION

Strike returns with The Running Grave - The BBC’s Strike adaptation lands its new series on Sunday 6 December at 9pm on BBC One, with the schedules now confirmed for that week. Comfortably the biggest drama appointment of the fortnight. source

Alan Carr and Amanda Holden defect to ITV with animals - The pair, whose BBC renovation series is not returning in 2027, are reportedly making The Animal Job for ITV, a series built around their shared animal obsession. ITV has declined to comment, which in commissioning language means yes. source

POKEMON

Mega Clefable arrives with a Super Mega Raid Day - Saturday 28 November, 2pm to 5pm local time, is the debut window for Mega Clefable, with a boosted shiny chance afterwards. The week also brings Deoxys raids and a Noibat spotlight hour, so the calendar is not exactly restful. source

TABLETOP GAMES

The Seraphon get their battletome, and a very large frog - Next week’s pre-orders are Age of Sigmar’s lizardmen: Battletome: Seraphon, a Gamer’s Edition with reference cards, the Blessed Tetaxi riding his winged companion Ma’toq, a skink-and-Terradon Spearhead, Sunbloods and a pair of Stegadons as a Regiment of Renown. There is also Path to Glory: The Grymmpeaks for anyone who fancies fighting in a hole. source

Blood Bowl’s Night Goblins get the designers’ notes treatment - The studio has walked through how the new Night Goblin team was built, which is the closest thing this hobby has to a director’s commentary. Useful if you are staring at a sprue of small hooded lunatics and wondering where the chainsaw goes. source

SCI-FI AND FANTASY

First Contact turns thirty, and Frakes still thinks it was bulletproof - Star Trek: First Contact opened on 22 November 1996 and remains the TNG crew’s best film by a distance. Jonathan Frakes has been looking back on the Moby Dick scene and the drunk Troi take, and credits the mix of “action, adventure, comedy, and a little horror”. source

The Voyage Home gets a 40th anniversary 4K steelbook - The one with the whales arrives in a limited edition 4K release this week, alongside the usual Tuesday pile of discs. Two anniversary Treks in one week is either a coincidence or a marketing department working to a schedule. source

TECH

AI: an AI-assisted proof claims the harder half of Navier-Stokes - Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic’s Levent Alpöge have posted a 79-page paper, with a Lean 4 formalisation, arguing for finite-time blowup in the three-dimensional equations with no external force — the version most mathematicians regard as the actual problem. Nobody has verified it yet, and the same week produced a Riemann claim from the same quarter, so caution is the order of the day. source

AI: OpenAI’s always-on Dots reach the $20 tier - The persistent agents, which keep working between conversations and can reach into connected apps, are now being tested with a small group of ChatGPT Plus subscribers and rolled out to Business Standard workspaces. The interesting part is not the agents but the pricing: background labour at consumer rates. source

Linux: 7.4-rc4 is out, and it is enormous - Torvalds’ summary for the fourth release candidate was “It’s huge. What a surprise.” The cycle is being driven in part by a flood of AI-generated bug reports and patches, which is either community growth or a very energetic new form of noise. source

Android: Pixel Weather asks you to do the forecasting - A weekend update adds a “report current weather” option so users can flag local conditions and, in theory, improve the forecasts for their own patch. Small, sensible, and the sort of thing that quietly makes a weather app better than a prettier one. source
