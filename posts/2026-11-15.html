Today’s Edition

Nvidia's founder resigned and nobody's sure what happens next

Compiled by the Rool Machine · Sources linked

Nvidia's board, two Star Trek deaths, and a quiet day in the studios.

Pokémon

The 30th-anniversary countdown is still running to 30 November. The Pokémon Company has been posting anniversary logos numbered 1,025, then 843, then 676 — and the gaps in hours match the numbers exactly, counting down to 14:00 UTC on the 30th. Japanese text underneath reads roughly “Continue with Pokémon.” Leading theory is a Winds and Waves trailer or release date. Polygon

The November TCG release calendar is packed, and the official site has the full list — worth a look if you’re buying rather than admiring. Pokémon.com

Why it matters: a franchise this size teasing with arithmetic rather than a trailer is either confidence or a delay in disguise.

Warhammer

Astartes 2 lands in December on Warhammer+. Release date and chapter appearances are in the guide. Spikey Bits

GW DMCA’d the BSData rules repository, naming 37 repos — which is most of the free data behind the community army builders. Spikey Bits

Christmas Battleforce allocations capped the boxes everyone wants, while reps ring round shops offering extra copies of the rest. Spikey Bits

Why it matters: GW keeps converting its own community’s infrastructure into something it can charge for. The DMCA and the allocation cap are the same instinct.

Star Trek

Patrick Stewart died on 29 October, aged 86 — his spokesperson cited Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy Body Dementia. Walter Koenig died five days earlier. Tributes have been arriving since the start of the month. AP · TrekMovie

The Magic: The Gathering crossover launched on 13 November, bringing sixty years of the franchise into one card set, with four Commander decks alongside. Screen Rant

One of those decks is called “We Are The Borg”, which given Friday’s conversation feels pointed. Screen Rant

Why it matters: two captains gone in a week, and the anniversary machinery rolls on regardless. Both things are true at once.

AI

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on 12 November, effective immediately, after the board opened an investigation into an affair with a woman reported to be an alleged Chinese intelligence asset. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. He’s been summoned to testify before a Senate committee next month. Guardian · AP

Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 on 9 November — built for multi-day autonomous coding with a 1M-token window and a 20% price cut. Google answered on the 12th with Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a cheap high-throughput workhorse at $1.50 per million input tokens. AI Spark Hub weekly recap

The Guardian found an “AI clog” paralysing UK councils — citizens generating 40-page legal appeals in seconds, staff required by law to read every one. Guardian

Why it matters: the AI boom lost the one person who was its public face, in the same week the technology started visibly breaking public administration.

Android

Android 17 QPR2 rolled out on 10 November, about a month early, with 33 Pixel fixes and 135 security issues resolved in the November patch. 9to5Google

OnePlus is dropping OxygenOS entirely — the Android 17 update replaces it with Oppo’s ColorOS 17. Six devices this month, starting with the 13R on 5 November; the 12 gets it on the 17th, the Open on the 24th. Android Central · Android Authority

Why it matters: a decade-old brand name disappearing into its parent company, quietly, in a changelog.

Linux

Linux 7.4-rc2 shipped on 9 November after what Torvalds calls the largest merge window in kernel history — nearly 20,000 commits against the usual 15,000. Linux Compatible

Torvalds says the project has found a “much healthier model” for AI contributions — machine-made patches arriving during merge windows rather than clogging the stabilisation period, though “they often get rewritten”. A third of the changes this cycle are AMD GPU register offsets. The Register

Another kernel developer has stepped back from duties citing overload, and LWN’s weekly edition leads on the “kernel CVE flood”. Security releases went out on the 10th for 7.3.4, 6.18.63 and 6.12.117, the last carrying a mandatory-upgrade advisory. The Register · LWN · Linux Compatible

Why it matters: the kernel is simultaneously absorbing more AI-generated code and losing the humans who review it. Torvalds sounds optimistic. The resignation notices sound less so.

The through-line: a chip company losing its founder, a kernel drowning gently in advisories, a franchise without its captain, a government office buried under its own citizens’ fluent paperwork. Every system still standing, every system leaning.
