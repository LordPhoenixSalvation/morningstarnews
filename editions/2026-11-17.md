## UK NEWS

**Milburn's review is published, and it says 440,000 more young people need work** — The report trailed all week arrived this morning, and it is blunter than the pre-briefings suggested: a "complete system reset", a legal entitlement to support through a new Youth Opportunity Service in England, a named adviser within four months, and a target of getting the Neet rate from 13% down to around 5% in a decade. The money comes from young adults losing the Universal Credit health top-up and Pip, replaced by a Youth Allowance and an Additional Cost Allowance. Milburn's own arithmetic is the striking bit: for every £1 spent helping young people into work last year, the state spent £25 on youth benefits. He wants that at £2 to £1 by 2036. [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c38xzy980924o)

**The Met's rape unit kept a predator on the payroll** — A detective who abused women was allowed to keep working in a specialist rape unit while complaints stacked up around him, with the force's own systems recording the behaviour becoming more extreme rather than triggering an intervention. It is the most-read story on the BBC this morning, which tells you something about how the public reads these cases now — not as individual failures but as a pattern with a management structure attached. [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6kgq8d779lro)

## TECH

**AI** — GitHub has deprecated several Copilot models as of yesterday: Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Terra are gone, with Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol/Luna pointed at instead. If you have CI or agent workflows pinning a specific model, they may be silently falling back rather than failing, which is the worse of the two outcomes. [AI Agent Store](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent-news/this-week)

**Android** — Google's November update, which reached Pixels early and is still rolling out, is leaving cameras unusable on some devices running Android 17, alongside reports of broken always-on display, lock screens and notifications. The security patch is fine; the functional update is the problem. [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/android-17-november-update-leaving-pixel-cameras-completely-unusable/)

**Hardware** — ESP-IDF v6.1.1 shipped yesterday, and it is a bugfix release for 6.1 rather than a feature drop, but two of the fixes matter if you build anything: `esp_https_ota` now refuses plain HTTP unless you explicitly opt back in, and the ESP HTTP server had a Content-Length truncation fixed to prevent request smuggling — plus a new `HTTPD_MAX_REQ_BODY_LEN_KB` cap defaulting to 16MB, which will break anything posting larger bodies without a config change. There is also a PSRAM XIP boot failure fixed for ESP32-P4 applications built with 5.5.2–5.5.4. If you are on 5.5.x, this is the release that stops the `CHIP_LP_WDT_RESET` loop. [ESP-IDF releases](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/releases/tag/v6.1.1)
