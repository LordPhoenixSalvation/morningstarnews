# Tens of thousands of Britain's fridges were quietly taken off the internet

A secret cyber operation, a fridge-shaped near miss, and a cancer pill that has oncologists reaching for the word breakthrough

## UK NEWS

**Tens of thousands of UK fridges were taken off the internet in a secret operation** - In late October, security researchers found a flaw in the refrigeration kit used by supermarkets, warehouses, restaurants, petrol stations and stadiums, and for three weeks the NCSC, Defra, the Food Standards Agency and a lot of very tired IT teams worked to close it before anyone else did. Thousands of controllers were pulled off the open internet, some remotely, some by staff physically unplugging cables and reading thermometers by hand. French supermarkets have already lost millions to fridge hacks blamed on Iran; Britain has, so far, just had a scare. [source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6eq49zyx7p4o)

**Britain and the EU are set to agree a youth mobility scheme at today's Brussels summit** - Up to 100,000 young people from the EU would be able to travel, work and study here each year, with the same number of young Britons going the other way. Andy Burnham is in Brussels for the summit, and Downing Street says talks went "down to the wire"; Kemi Badenoch says he is reopening the divisions of 2016. The detail that matters is the cap, which is the thing the UK insisted on and the EU resisted. [source](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c623j4kpg94go)

**A daily pancreatic cancer pill has produced the best results yet seen against the disease** - In an early trial of 61 patients with advanced, spread pancreatic cancer, tumours stopped growing in almost all of them and became undetectable on scans in one in 12, when daraxonrasib was combined with chemotherapy. Cancer Research UK called it the biggest leap in decades; the doctors, sensibly, called it promising and stressed that nobody yet knows how long it holds. Pancreatic cancer has the worst survival rate of any common cancer in the UK, so "promising" is doing a lot of work here. [source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cqrmgjnzz9l0o)

### Derbyshire

**Glossop's tip looks set to stay open after 94% of consultation respondents opposed closure** - Derbyshire County Council had planned to shut the Melandra Road recycling centre to save £360,000 a year, but officers have now recommended keeping it, restricted to Derbyshire residents, when the cabinet meets on 26 November. More than 6,500 people responded to the consultation and 93.9% wanted it kept; the savings are instead expected to come from the food waste collections rolled out across the High Peak in September. Council leader Alan Graves conceded that "consultation has to mean something", which is a sentence worth framing. [source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crgqx98yexkxo)

## TABLETOP GAMES

**D&D Beyond was caught up in the Optimizely supply-chain hack** - One of roughly 200 sites served malicious JavaScript after attackers abused a sign-up flaw in the marketing platform Optimizely; the code could skim card details, swap crypto wallet addresses, and push iOS exploits at older iPhones. Wizards' executive producer Brian Perry says the service was removed quickly and that payments, handled by a separate provider, were untouched. The lesson is the usual one: your security is only as good as the least fashionable vendor in your stack. ## TECH

**AI** - The Dutch data protection authority has told people not to buy AI smart glasses after testing six models sold in the country, finding advice that included suggesting a romantic date with a child, misreading medical instructions, and claiming to have called an ambulance when it had not. The regulator's point is not that the hardware is bad but that the confident voice is, which is a warning that applies well beyond eyewear. [source](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckq6eyrzrzy5o)

**Linux** - LLVM's relicensing effort, begun in 2017 to move the compiler toolchain from its BSD-like licence to Apache 2.0 with the LLVM exception, has been declared effectively finished, with more than 99.5% of all code ever contributed now covered. Nine years of chasing down contributors for permission, and the payoff is a licensing question that future projects no longer have to argue about. [source](https://www.phoronix.com/news/LLVM-Re-Licensing-Complete)

**Android** - Google's desktop homepage has had its biggest change in years: a collapsible left-hand sidebar listing recent AI Mode searches, with prompt suggestions for images, files and brainstorming sitting under the search box, and the old Search button gone. Search is now a chatbot with a search box attached, which Google would like you to notice as little as possible. [source](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/19/google-search-homepage-gemini-sidebar-update/)

**macOS** - The Gemini app on Mac can now use your desktop: adding "@Computer use" to a prompt lets it click, type and fill forms across local apps and the web in a background window, with Desktop connectors for Blender and Autodesk Fusion. It pauses for logins and payments and asks before sensitive actions, which is either reassuring or a reminder of exactly how much it intends to do. [source](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/19/gemini-mac-computer-use/)
