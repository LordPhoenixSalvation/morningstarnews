# Burnham comes back from Brussels with a deal, and the Brexit argument restarts

Britain signs a post-Brexit reset in Brussels, a rape case reopens the consent debate, and a cancer pill does something genuinely startling in a trial.

## UK NEWS

**Burnham hails a "historic" EU deal as Badenoch calls it unfair** - The prime minister signed agreements in Brussels covering food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme allowing up to 100,000 young EU citizens a year to live, work and study in the UK, with the same number of Britons going the other way. The Treasury reckons the package is worth up to £17bn a year. Legislation lands in Parliament next week, which is when the arguments really begin. [source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6jreyqgw4e9o)

**Calls to change the consent defence after the Joanne Young verdict** - Campaigners and MPs want the law tightened so a defendant can no longer argue he reasonably believed consent existed where it plainly did not. The case has done what these cases occasionally do: moved a legal argument out of the seminar room and into the news. [source](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqy4jvprxyrdo)

**A pancreatic cancer pill that made tumours vanish** - Early trial results reported today describe complete responses in patients with what is normally a death sentence within months. "Massive hope" is the phrase being used, and for once it is not being used loosely - though this is a trial, not a treatment, and the usual caveats about size and follow-up apply. [source](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqrmgjnzz9l0o)

### Derbyshire

**EMR puts Sunday morning trains back on the Crewe-Derby-Nottingham line** - From 13 December, Sunday services on that route start around 09:00 instead of about 14:00, and Lincoln to Sleaford gets a Sunday service for the first time since the 1970s. Modest stuff, but the kind of timetable change that quietly alters whether people can take a job or visit family. [source](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck20vlj5yjj1o)

## SCI-FI AND FANTASY

**Star Trek's novel line has no 2027 schedule at all** - Una McCormack's Deep Space Nine novel The Peacemakers came out on 17 November, and Pocket Books' catalogue lists nothing after it - no follow-up, no 2027 titles, no freelancers contracted. An author says the licence has not been renewed. After 47 years of continuous publishing, silence is a loud noise. [source](https://www.fandompulse.com/p/star-treks-novel-line-has-no-2027)

## TECH

**AI: Gemini 4 Neon arrives, Google's fourth Gemini 4 model in under two months** - Launched on 18 November with frontier-level reasoning at Flash-ish speeds, it replaces Argon for AI Pro subscribers and undercuts rivals on API price. The release cadence has become the story: Google is now shipping flagship-tier models faster than most people can finish evaluating the last one. [source](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-launches-gemini-4-neon-with-opus-5-5-level-coding-performance-at-25-lower-api-token-prices)

**Linux: 7.5 will let you block /proc/kcore** - The kernel is adding an option to close off /proc/kcore, the pseudo-file that exposes physical memory and is therefore a gift to anyone who gets root. A small, sensible hardening knob that distributions will almost certainly turn on by default. [source](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-7.5-Restrict-proc-kcore)

**Android: App Lock comes back to Pixel in beta** - Google has re-released the feature in Android 17 QPR3 Beta 3, and Samsung Galaxy devices are getting it too. The idea - a second lock on chosen apps, independent of the screen lock - has taken long enough to arrive that third-party apps built a business on it. [source](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/18/google-pixel-app-lock-release-beta/)
