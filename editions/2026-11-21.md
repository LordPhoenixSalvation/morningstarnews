## TECH

**AI: Anthropic's market debut raised $81bn, the largest IPO on record** - Shares began trading this week at a valuation near $1.5 trillion, minting a small forest of new millionaires and putting public shareholders behind a company whose pitch has always been about restraint. The safety-first lab now has a share price to defend. [source](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/19/2026/anthropic-makes-record-market-debut)

**AI: Meta's Muse has been mixing up other people's conversations** - A report says employees testing the assistant were shown unfamiliar childcare messages, reminders and bank balances. Meta says staff were trying unreleased features. Either way, it is a useful reminder of what an agent with memory can get wrong. [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/metas-ai-agent-muse-is-mixing-up-conversations-and-exposing-private-data-report-says/)

**Linux: a critical bug in GnuPG's GPGME is reaching mail clients and Flatpak** - The library many applications use to talk to GPG has a serious security flaw, and the affected surface is wider than the library itself. Patch early; encrypted mail that does not encrypt is worse than none. [source](https://www.phoronix.com/news/GnuPG-GPGME-Critical)

**Android: Google is retiring the Nest app and Assistant on speakers and displays** - Google Home takes over from the Nest app in January, and Assistant disappears from smart speakers and screens during 2027, completing a deprecation that started on phones in September. Anyone still saying "Hey Google" to a Nest Hub has about a year of habit left. [source](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/20/nest-app-google-assistant/)

**macOS: Gemini can now drive your Mac** - Google's agent can organise local files, fill in forms and operate apps while you keep working, initially for US subscribers. Apple is reportedly building its own version. Letting software click things on your behalf is now a product category, and the permissions warnings are coming with it. [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/google-gives-gemini-the-ability-to-use-your-mac-for-you/)

The week's lesson, if there is one: everyone wants to be trusted with your data, and nobody wants to be audited. The ICO has discovered that about its own filing cabinets, Meta about its test conversations, and quite possibly you about the AI agent you are about to let click "confirm".
